# BotStrike art direction and reproducible previews

The 2050 cover is the art reference: six industrial robots, cyan/mint/amber/violet/magenta/orange identity colors, worn segmented armor, emissive sensors, rainy freight infrastructure and a layered night skyline. The cover was generated with the built-in image_gen tool; its exact prompt and original source are retained in `botstrike-art.json` and `../assets/botstrike-source/keyart.png`.

The six gameplay chassis adapt the Sotudios Humanoid Robot Pack under CC BY 4.0. Source `.blend` files, the vendor manifest and license records are in `../assets/characters/`; the shipped, rigged GLBs and external textures are in `../games/botstrike/web/models/`. BotStrike adds role colors, equipment and 25 animation clips. The first-person arms come from those same skinned meshes.

Weapons, equipment and environments use original Three.js geometry in `games/botstrike/client/art-direction.mjs`, not a flat cover projected into gameplay. The armory and carried weapon share that factory. ARC-9 has a compact twin-cell housing; NEEDLE uses a long vented accelerator shroud; SCATTER uses a wide muzzle, heat shroud and ribbed pump. SCATTER's pump follows the existing fire event in both views and remains still under reduced motion. Weapon geometry is presentation only; native combat and replay rules remain independent of it.

The three map environments share the industrial palette but have different infrastructure: docks and freight cranes, antenna towers and rooftop decks, and reactor vessels/pipes. Only visual dressing is added to native collision primitives. Detail within walkable areas is flat material work; large decorative structures are outside the arena. The shared map specification still defines all movement and shot collision.

Selection portraits, armory thumbnails and map cards are renders of the gameplay models and geometry. `tests/botstrike-art.mjs` builds the development-only preview harness, renders six heroes, twelve weapons and three maps, checks draw/triangle budgets and checks that repeated map switches release GPU resources. It does not ship development globals. Convert those outputs to the committed WebP previews after changing models. The cover is cinematic concept art; the live game is a stylized real-time rendition, and no claim of pixel-identical or photoreal cinematic fidelity is made.

Rendering bounds backing resolution to 8,294,400 pixels and device ratio to 1.5, merges static meshes by material, caches environment and static shadow maps, and turns off rain for reduced motion or reduced resolution. Opaque menus suspend scene rendering. Shader warmup happens before the session is declared ready, avoiding an initial compilation stall being mistaken for a lost connection. Browser checks are software-rendered Chromium evidence, not measured physical mobile GPU performance.
